The HR Stack Guide research team evaluated 2026 feature sets and pricing models for Rippling and Gusto for Seed to Series B fast-growing technology companies hiring remote engineers worldwide. Based on that analysis, Gusto is the recommended choice for Domestic SMB Simplicity. Rippling starts at $8/user/mo while Gusto starts at $49/mo + $6/user.

If your workforce is US-based and you want payroll, tax filings, and benefits to run themselves, Gusto is the stronger call on Domestic SMB Simplicity for Tech Startups: automated federal, state, and local filings come standard with the cleanest admin experience for lean teams. If instead you want HR, payroll, and IT device provisioning running off one employee record, Rippling is the better shortlist candidate: its workflow automation spans onboarding, app access, and hardware provisioning in a single system.

Based on The HR Stack Guide's 2026 evaluation, Gusto is the stronger fit for Domestic SMB Simplicity for Tech Startups evaluating international remote team operations: Best-in-class US payroll experience purpose-built for small teams, without added IT device-management overhead; Automated state-by-state tax filings come standard, reducing the compliance workload for lean SMB teams; and Simple, transparent per-employee pricing starting lower than a base-fee-plus-per-user model. Both platforms carry solid overall ratings (4.6/5 for Gusto vs. 4.8/5 for Rippling), but Rippling is built primarily as a general-purpose domestic HRIS, so Gusto pulls ahead specifically on Domestic SMB Simplicity for globally distributed teams.

Modeled three-year platform escalation for this Rippling versus Gusto hub puts Rippling near $4,645 in year three against $26,672 for Gusto, using published seat math rather than a sales-deck average.

Rippling starts at $8/user/mo (Core platform from $8/user/mo; payroll and EOR are modular add-ons) and is tagged Best for Multi-State Scaling. Gusto starts at $49/mo + $6/user (US payroll Simple plan; no international EOR) and is tagged Best for US SMBs (1–50). Ratings in this file are 4.8 versus 4.6.
